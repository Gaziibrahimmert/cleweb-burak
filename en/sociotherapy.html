Sociotherapy

Support for more stability and independence

Mental illness can make everyday life considerably harder. Sometimes it is difficult to structure the day, keep appointments or organise necessary treatment.

This is where sociotherapy comes in.

We support you in shaping your everyday life more self-determinedly, using your personal strengths and developing new perspectives. Your individual needs and goals are at the centre.

Goals

Goals of sociotherapy

Sociotherapy helps people with severe mental illness strengthen their independence and make use of necessary medical treatment. At the same time, hospital stays should be avoided or shortened wherever possible.

Further goals include:

  • Promoting mental stability and recognising crises early
  • Strengthening independence and initiative
  • Activating personal skills and resources
  • Gaining more confidence in daily life and coping better with challenges
  • Fostering social skills and contacts
  • Improving quality of life and enabling participation in society

Support

How does sociotherapy support you?

Sociotherapeutic support is tailored to your personal situation. Together we develop practical solutions that help you manage everyday life better.

Our support includes:

  • Individual support planning and jointly setting personal goals
  • Conversations about stress, fears and personal challenges
  • Developing a suitable daily and weekly structure
  • Developing strategies for dealing with crises and difficult situations
  • Motivation and accompaniment to medical and therapy appointments
  • Fostering social contacts and help with using and coordinating further support services

Who it is for

Who is sociotherapy suitable for?

Sociotherapy is for people with severe mental illness who, because of their illness, find it hard to access necessary medical or therapeutic services on their own.

Sociotherapy can be particularly helpful if:

  • planning and organising everyday life independently is significantly impaired,
  • anxiety or other mental strain makes it hard to leave home,
  • medical or therapy appointments cannot be kept without support,
  • additional mental or physical illnesses increase the need for support.

Whether sociotherapy is an option is assessed on the basis of your individual medical circumstances.

Duration

Duration of sociotherapy

With the appropriate medical indication, people with statutory health insurance can receive up to 120 therapy units of sociotherapy within three years.

The frequency and length of sessions depend on your individual needs and the goals we agree on together.

Referral

Who can prescribe sociotherapy?

Under certain conditions, sociotherapy can be prescribed by the following professionals and institutions:

  • Specialists in psychiatry and psychotherapy, neurology, and psychosomatic medicine and psychotherapy
  • Medical and psychological psychotherapists
  • Authorised child and adolescent psychotherapists
  • Authorised psychiatric outpatient clinics

GPs and other contracted doctors can also, under certain conditions, issue a referral to assess whether sociotherapy is suitable. Up to five sociotherapy units are provided for this.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long does sociotherapy last?

Up to 120 hours are available within three years. How much of that is needed depends on your goals. Hours are prescribed in stages and reviewed together regularly.

Can I get in touch as a relative?

Yes. Often it is relatives who make the first contact. Sociotherapy itself, however, requires that the person concerned agrees and takes part.

Do sessions only take place at the practice?

No. Part of the work happens wherever it is needed – at your home, on the way to the doctor or at a public office, for example.

What happens to my information?

Everything you share with me is subject to professional confidentiality. I only coordinate with your doctor within the scope of treatment and with your consent.

I don't have a prescription – what now?

Call anyway. I will explain who can prescribe in your situation and what to bring to the appointment.
